Hindi fonts for apps, menus and mixed Hindi-English text

This last page has clean sans-serif fonts: Yantramanav, Laila, Ranga, Dekko, Gotu, Sumana, Cambay, Akshar, Poppins and Noto Sans. Most are simple and neutral, which suits app screens, menus, captions and social media posts.

When Hindi and English appear together

Mixed text is where fonts can look uneven. English letters can look larger or smaller than Hindi letters, and the baseline can seem to jump. To check this, type a line such as Aaj ka special: मसाला चाय in the box above and compare the fonts.

  • Yantramanav and Poppins are good to test first for mixed lines.
  • If one font looks uneven, try another from this page before you change the size.
  • Keep both languages in the same font family where possible.

Fallback fonts in your own design

If a font does not have a certain character, the browser shows that character in the next font on your list. That is why a font stack is useful:

body { font-family: "Poppins", "Noto Sans Devanagari", sans-serif; }

Here Poppins is used first, and Noto Sans Devanagari takes over for any Hindi letters that Poppins cannot show.

Small-screen tips

  • Avoid very light weights for small text on a phone.
  • Use at least 16 px for body text.
  • Leave room between lines, because matras need space.
  • Test your text on a real phone, not only on a computer screen.
