What this converter does

Hinglish is Hindi written with English letters, the way most of us type on a phone. This tool reads those letters by sound and writes the same words in Devanagari, so mera naam Ali hai becomes मेरा नाम अली है. Some people search for this as English to Hindi typing or Roman to Hindi.

It is not a translator. It never changes the meaning of a word, so pyaar becomes प्यार, while the English word love becomes लव. The result is ordinary Unicode text, which means it can be searched, copied and shown by WhatsApp, Instagram, Word, Google Docs and any website. It is not Kruti Dev text.

The typing rules

The tool follows how a word sounds and lets you control the vowel length. A short a inside a word stays silent, so kal becomes कल. A long vowel needs two letters, so kaal becomes काल. That one habit fixes most spelling surprises.

Half letters. Two consonants typed together join only where Hindi usually joins them, as in dost (दोस्त), kya (क्या) and bachche (बच्चे). In other places the tool keeps the short a, so karna gives करना and sakte gives सकते.

If it guesses wrong, type an a to keep two letters apart, or a + to join them.

The letter n before k, g, ch, j, t, d, p, b or s becomes the dot ं, so hindi gives हिंदी and sundar gives सुंदर. Before p and b, the letter m does the same, so lamba gives लंबा.

Four habits for correct spelling

  1. Double the long vowels. kam gives कम and kaam gives काम. The tool cannot hear the difference, so you tell it.
  2. Trust the word endings. A final a becomes ा and a final i becomes ी, which is right for most everyday words such as mera, kya and hindi. If a word should end in ि or end silently, as some Sanskrit words do, switch that option off.
  3. Split or join with a and +. Add a to keep two consonants apart and + to force a half letter, as in dhar+m for धर्म.
  4. Keep English words separate. Put a whole English phrase in curly braces to leave it unchanged, or type loan words the way Hindi speakers say them, such as time for टाइम.

Why Hinglish spelling varies

Hinglish has no official spelling. One person writes kya, another writes kyaa, and mein may appear as mai, main or me. Hindi also has sounds that English letters do not show, such as the difference between त and ट, or the vowel length in किताब.

No converter can read your mind, so this one follows one fixed set of rules. That makes the result predictable: once you know the rules above, you can fix any word in a few seconds.

What it cannot do

  • It does not translate. It only writes the sound of what you typed.
  • It cannot know vowel length from a Roman spelling, so you type aa, ee and oo yourself.
  • It does not cover every Sanskrit or Marathi letter. For example ळ and ऋ are not typed with simple letters.
  • It does not produce Kruti Dev or other font-based text.
  • It may guess a half letter wrong in rare words. Use a or + to correct it.

Questions people ask

Is this a translator?

No. It writes the sound of your Roman letters in Devanagari. It does not turn English meaning into Hindi meaning.

Why is my word spelled slightly wrong?

Hindi vowel length cannot be guessed from Roman spelling, because kitab could be several different words. Type kitaab for किताब and bhaarat for भारत. Use a to keep two consonants apart and + to join them.

Will the font stay when I paste the text into another app?

No. Copy gives plain Unicode text, and the app you paste into shows it in its own font. To keep a style, download the PNG, or install the font from its Google Fonts page.

Is my text stored or sent anywhere?

The conversion and the PNG are made inside your browser, and the text is not saved on a server. Your browser does load the fonts from Google Fonts, as it does on the main generator. The Privacy Policy explains this in full.

Does it work for Marathi, Nepali or Sanskrit?

The rules are made for Hindi. Many Marathi, Nepali and Sanskrit words come out correctly because they use the same script, but some letters, such as ळ, are not included.

The fonts in this tool come from Google Fonts and are published under open licences. For a large commercial project, read the licence on the font's Google Fonts page first.
