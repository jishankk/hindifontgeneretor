DMCA Policy

Last updated: September 19, 2026

HindiFontGen.in ("we", "us", "our") respects the intellectual property rights of others and expects our users to do the same. This page explains our policy and process under the Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA) for handling claims of copyright infringement.

1. Our Approach to Copyright

HindiFontGen is a browser-based Hindi font preview tool. The text you type into the tool is processed entirely on your own device — it is never uploaded to, stored on, or displayed publicly from our servers. Because of this, our site does not host user-generated content, image galleries, or file uploads in the way many other websites do.

The typefaces displayed on this site are sourced from Google Fonts, and each is distributed under an open font license (typically the SIL Open Font License) that permits this kind of use. If you believe any content on this website — including text, images, code, or design elements that we do host and control — infringes a copyright you own, this policy tells you how to notify us.

2. Filing a DMCA Takedown Notice

If you are a copyright owner, or an agent authorised to act on a copyright owner's behalf, and you believe that material appearing on HindiFontGen.in infringes your copyright, you may submit a written takedown notice to our designated agent. Under 17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3), a valid notice must include the following:

  1. A physical or electronic signature of a person authorised to act on behalf of the copyright owner.
  2. Identification of the copyrighted work claimed to have been infringed, or, if multiple works are covered by a single notice, a representative list of such works.
  3. Identification of the material that is claimed to be infringing, with enough detail (such as a specific URL on our site) to allow us to locate it.
  4. Your contact information, including at minimum an address, telephone number, and email address.
  5. A statement that you have a good-faith belief that the disputed use is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
  6. A statement, made under penalty of perjury, that the information in your notice is accurate and that you are the copyright owner or authorised to act on the owner's behalf.

Important: Under Section 512(f) of the DMCA, anyone who knowingly and materially misrepresents that material is infringing may be liable for damages. Please be certain of your claim before submitting a notice.

4. What Happens After We Receive a Notice

Upon receiving a complete and valid notice, we will:

  • Review the notice to confirm it satisfies the requirements listed in Section 2 above.
  • Remove or disable access to the identified material that is within our control, where the claim appears valid.
  • Make a good-faith attempt to notify the party responsible for the content, where applicable, that the material has been removed or disabled and why.

Incomplete notices — for example, those missing a signature, a specific URL, or a good-faith statement — may delay our ability to act and may be returned to the sender for correction.

5. Filing a Counter-Notice

If material you posted or that you believe was wrongly removed has been taken down as a result of a mistake or misidentification, you may submit a counter-notice under 17 U.S.C. § 512(g)(3). A valid counter-notice must include:

  1. Your physical or electronic signature.
  2. Identification of the material that was removed or disabled, and its location before removal.
  3. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that you have a good-faith belief the material was removed as a result of mistake or misidentification.
  4. Your name, address, and telephone number, and a statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the federal district court for the judicial district in which your address is located (or, if outside the United States, for any judicial district in which we may be found), and that you will accept service of process from the person who filed the original takedown notice or their agent.

If we receive a valid counter-notice, we may forward it to the original complaining party. Under the DMCA, the original complainant then has the opportunity to seek a court order restraining the alleged infringer from engaging in the infringing activity. If we receive no such notice of legal action within a reasonable time, we may, at our discretion, restore the removed material.

6. Repeat Infringers

Where applicable to our site's functionality, we reserve the right, in appropriate circumstances, to disable or terminate access for users who are found to be repeat infringers of copyright.
