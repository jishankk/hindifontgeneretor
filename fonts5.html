Readable Hindi fonts for websites, apps and documents

Asar, Martel and Annapurna SIL are book-style fonts. Hind, Mukta, Noto Sans Devanagari, Anek Devanagari, Palanquin, Palanquin Dark and Martel Sans are clean sans-serif fonts. They are made for everyday reading, not for decoration.

What makes a Hindi font easy to read

  • Clear matras. The signs above and below the letters should be distinct, even at a small size.
  • Even stroke weight. Very thin or very heavy fonts tire the eyes in long text.
  • Open shapes. Letters like ख, घ and ध should not look closed or blurred.
  • Good support for joined letters. Test words such as क्ष, ज्ञ and श्र before you choose a font.

Practical settings for a website

  • Body text size: around 16 to 18 px on mobile and desktop. Hindi usually needs to be a little larger than English to feel equally easy.
  • Line height: 1.6 or more for paragraphs.
  • Fonts per page: two at most, and only the weights you actually use. This keeps the page fast.
  • Always add a fallback so text still shows if the font does not load.

For example, in CSS you can write:

body { font-family: "Hind", "Noto Sans Devanagari", sans-serif; }

Unicode versus old Hindi fonts

All fonts on this site use Unicode, which is the standard that works on websites, phones and search engines. Older fonts such as Kruti Dev store text differently. If you have Kruti Dev text and need Unicode, use the Unicode and Kruti Dev converter.
