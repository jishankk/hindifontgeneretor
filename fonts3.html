Making posters, thumbnails and banners with bold Hindi fonts

Bakbak One, Sansita, Arya, Karma, Khand, Teko, Rajdhani, Sarpanch, Biryani and Jaldi are made to be seen from a distance. They are better for short, strong text than for long paragraphs.

Use the preview to test your actual headline. A font that looks great with two words can become crowded with eight.

How to get a strong result

  • Keep it short. Three to six words is usually enough for a poster or thumbnail headline.
  • Pick one bold font and stay with it. Mixing two heavy fonts in the same design makes both look messy.
  • Use high contrast. Light text on a dark background, or the other way round. Use the colour pickers to try a few combinations.
  • Use condensed fonts for long titles. Khand, Teko and Jaldi fit more letters in a narrow space.
  • Use rounded fonts for a friendly feel. Biryani and Bakbak One look softer than squared fonts like Rajdhani.

Effects: when they help and when they do not

The tool has outline, shadow, glow, 3D and gradient effects. A light shadow can help text stay readable over a busy photo. Glow works on dark backgrounds. Outline and 3D look best on very heavy fonts and can ruin thin ones. If you are unsure, use no effect and let the font do the work.

Common mistakes

  • Text that is too small to read on a phone screen. Always check how it looks at a small size.
  • Low contrast, such as yellow text on white.
  • Stretching the text after downloading it. Change the size in the tool instead, so the letters stay sharp.
  • Using too many effects at once.
